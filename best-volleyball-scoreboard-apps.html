Best Volleyball Scoreboard Apps.

A scoreboard has one job: everyone in the gym knows the score. Here is how the main options handle that, and what each one gives up to do it.

We make one of these apps. VolleyRef.App is ours, so treat what we say about it accordingly. Everything stated about the other apps comes from their own store listing, website, or repository, and the sections below say what each one is genuinely better at.

What Matters

What Makes a Good Scoreboard App?

Readable Across the Gym

A scoreboard nobody can read from the back row is a phone with numbers on it. Big type, high contrast, and a display that survives daylight.

Spectators Without Installs

Parents will not download an app to check a score. A link that opens in any browser reaches everyone; an app-store listing reaches almost nobody.

Stream and TV Output

Casting to a gym TV or dropping a live overlay into OBS, vMix, or Streamlabs turns the scoreboard into part of the broadcast.

Survives a Dead Connection

Gym wifi fails. A scoreboard that stops when the connection drops is worse than a flip chart, which at least keeps working.

Knows the Rules

Most scoreboards count taps. A few understand rotation, substitutions, and the libero, and refuse to record something illegal.

Leaves a Record

Some matches need a scoresheet afterwards. Whether the app can produce one decides if you also keep paper.

The Alternatives

The Competition.

Volleyball Score Simple

A free, ad-free tap counter for iOS and Android by Patrick Wespi. Its own listing describes it as a simple and clean score keeper with big tap boxes and device sync.

Genuinely Free

Completely free with no ads, which nothing else here matches. If all you need is the score, this is the shortest path to it.

Very Easy to Hand Over

Large tap targets and almost no interface. Anyone on the bench can run it without being taught.

Counts, Does Not Officiate

It tracks points and sets. Rotation, substitution limits, and the libero are yours to remember.

VS Score (VolleyStation)

VolleyStation's scorekeeping app, which its site describes as replacing the paper scoresheet with a digital one for junior volleyball and simplifying event operations.

Built for Event Operators

Aimed at tournaments and clubs, gathering scores across an event and sharing data with coaches and parents rather than serving one match.

Digital Scoresheet

Replaces the paper print for organisers who need results collected centrally, which is a real gap for a multi-court event.

Less About the Gym Wall

It is a scorekeeping and event tool first. If your problem is that nobody courtside can see the score, that is not what it is for.

VolleyScore

An open-source project on GitHub (Reccardenee/VolleyScore) described by its own README as a lightweight web-based scoreboard overlay for volleyball broadcasts using OBS Studio, with a control panel for scores, sets, and service indicators.

Free and Fully Yours

Open source, so you can change anything. For a broadcaster who wants an overlay styled exactly their way, that is worth a lot.

You Host and Run It

It is a repository, not a product. Someone has to set it up, serve it, and keep it running on match day.

Overlay, Not Match Record

It drives the on-screen scoreboard. Rules, scoresheets, and a shareable spectator link are outside its scope.

VBScore

An Apple-ecosystem scoreboard from vbscore.ca for volleyball, pickleball, and rally sports. Its site describes scoring from Apple Watch with the score shown large on iPhone or iPad, broadcasting to spectators, and recording video with the score built in.

Best Apple Watch Setup

Scoring from the wrist while an iPhone or iPad stays on the sideline as the display is a genuinely good two-device arrangement.

Video With the Score Burned In

Recording match video with the scoreboard already composited, and streaming to YouTube, is a neat trick for club footage.

Apple Only

No Android and no plain browser. Anyone on the crew without an Apple device is out, which for a volunteer bench is a real constraint.

FAQ

Common Questions.

What is the best volleyball scoreboard app?

It depends on the job. For a free tap counter, Volleyball Score Simple. For Apple Watch scoring, VBScore. For collecting results across a tournament, VS Score. For a scoreboard that enforces the rules, shares to spectators by link, and drops into a stream, VolleyRef.App.

Can I put a volleyball scoreboard on my stream?

Yes. VolleyRef.App gives you a URL you add as a Browser Source in OBS, vMix, or Streamlabs, with nothing to host. VolleyScore is an open-source OBS overlay you run yourself. VBScore can record or stream video with the score composited in.

How do spectators see the score without installing anything?

Share the VolleyRef.App live link. It opens in any browser with no install and no account, and the same link can be cast to a TV in the gym.

Do volleyball scoreboard apps work without wifi?

VolleyRef.App is offline-first, so a full match scores with no connection and devices in the gym sync directly, then resync to the cloud later. Most simple counters work offline because they never leave the device, but that also means nobody outside the gym sees the score.

Is a scoreboard app enough, or do I still need a scoresheet?

If your league wants a scoresheet, a plain scoreboard will not produce one. VolleyRef.App exports an official PDF the moment the match ends, and VS Score offers a digital scoresheet for events. The others are display only.
